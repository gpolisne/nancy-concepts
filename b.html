Priorities

Four fights that decide the next decade.

Public education

Arizona has been defunding public schools since the 1990s, when districts were forced to choose between PE, music, and art, and which students would get them.

Buildings are falling apart. Many schools have no reliable air conditioning. There are classrooms unusable because of asbestos and no money in the state budget to fix them. Bonds and overrides cannot raise enough. Infrastructure money has to come from the state.

Reproductive freedom

Everyone deserves access to education about their own body, to birth control, to pre and post natal care, and to quality health care. Arizona has money in its budget to fund care for women and children who cannot afford insurance.

I will fight until my daughters, my students, and everyone who is pregnant has the right to decide what happens to their own body. That is not a place for government.

Gun legislation

I introduced a bill this session to repeal the preemption that stops Arizona cities from regulating firearms in their own communities.

Every time an announcement comes over the intercom, part of me wonders whether this is the time I have to defend my students against an active shooter. That is not okay, and I will keep bringing legislation forward until it changes.

Water

We cannot just hope there will be enough, because the truth is there will not be. I will work with water experts on policy that conserves what we have and finds better ways to cut the enormous volume used by agriculture.

The time to address this aggressively is now, so Arizona and the people in it can keep living here.

Background

Thirty years in Arizona classrooms.

Nancy attended Arizona public schools from kindergarten through college at NAU, where she earned a degree in elementary education with an emphasis in math. She has held an Arizona teaching credential since 1994 and taught in Paradise Valley, Sierra Vista, and at Tucson High from 2013 to 2025.

She has lived across the state, including Phoenix, Flagstaff, and Sierra Vista as a military spouse. She now lives in Tucson with her husband Andy. Their daughters Megan and Macie went through Arizona public schools their whole lives.

She served on the board of Tucson NOW and as its president in 2018, volunteered as a Red Cross volunteer coordinator while her family was stationed at Fort Drum, and completed 500 hours of certified yoga teacher training before bringing teaching and yoga together at Tucson High. She is a proud union member.
